# Worked direction: a field-notes archive

## Direction
Example brief: readers compare observations from different places, inspect a few striking photographs, and read detailed notes. The intended character is curious, tactile and careful. The main task is comparison followed by reading; the product is not a generic subscription landing page.

Choose a field-notebook direction: off-white paper-like canvas, dark ink, one restrained annotation color, an expressive but readable title face and a neutral body face. The exact colors and fonts are choices to verify on the actual content, not universally correct tokens. Reject a dark neon dashboard here because it makes the collection feel like live telemetry; reject scattered scrapbook rotations because they interfere with comparison.

[Develop competing directions before choosing](knowledge:caliper/guides/ui/direction#brief-to-direction)

## Composition
Give the newest or most relevant observation a dominant image and an adjacent concise note. Place the archive as aligned rows with date, location, subject and a short description. Use thumbnails only where they help selection. Keep filters next to the collection they change, and show the active filter and result count.

A weak version wraps every record in a large rounded card, gives all images equal weight and fills the header with an oversized slogan. It consumes the first screen without helping readers choose. Improve it by reserving the largest image for the featured observation, using rows for comparison and giving the title only the space its content requires.

[Choose layout from the user's comparison task](knowledge:caliper/guides/ui/layout#sequence)

## Type and space
Use title typography to convey the archive's voice, body typography for sustained reading and small metadata that remains comfortably legible. Group a date and location with their record; separate records more strongly than fields within a record. Keep captions attached to their image. Use a consistent baseline and line-length rhythm rather than decorative labels everywhere.

The narrow version places the featured image above its note and reduces metadata to a readable sequence. It preserves all essential information rather than hiding dates behind hover.

[Build and verify the type scale](knowledge:caliper/guides/ui/typography#hierarchy)

## Assets
Use photographs selected for the actual observations, with a consistent crop policy and honest captions. If photographs are unavailable, use restrained original diagrams that explain the observation; do not substitute irrelevant stock scenes. One small annotation motif can connect captions and references. Avoid paper textures that reduce text contrast or pretend to be source evidence.

## Acceptance
Inspect a long note, a missing image, an empty filter result, a selected record and narrow layout. Verify filter controls change the visible collection and clearing them restores it. Ask whether the result feels like a specific archive with a reading purpose, not whether it resembles the example's colors. This example is a method for deriving coherent choices, not a template to copy.
