# Composing a useful surface

## Sequence
List the user's main questions in order: where am I, what matters now, what can I do, and what happened? Arrange content to answer that sequence. For a comparison task, shared axes and aligned rows often beat separate cards. For a story, a deliberate sequence of image, claim and evidence may work better than equal tiles. For an editor, keep the object, controls and consequences spatially connected.

Sketch the silhouette before details: dominant region, supporting region and quiet space. Give the primary region enough weight to be unmistakable. Asymmetry can establish direction, but random offsets create noise. Use alignment to connect related elements across the page. Repeated vertical centers and three identical cards often flatten different meanings into one template.

## Grid and density
Choose columns and spacing from actual content and interactions. Use a small spacing scale, then apply it relationally: tighter within a group, larger between groups, largest between major sections. A card is appropriate when the group has an independent identity or action. Otherwise use typography, whitespace and rules. Bordering every element makes all boundaries compete.

Density should match the task. A monitoring surface may need many values in view; a reading surface needs comfortable measure. Do not confuse empty acreage with quality, or dense information with clutter. Remove repeated explanatory text and redundant labels before hiding essential controls. Let long content wrap and containers grow where the task allows it.

## Responsive composition
Define what changes when space contracts: columns become rows, secondary details move behind an explicit affordance, or controls reorganize around the same primary action. Preserve the relationship between a control and its feedback. Do not simply shrink the desktop composition until labels collide.

Test the longest plausible content at a few meaningful widths, including just before and after layout transitions. Inspect overflow, overlapping sticky regions, unreachable actions, keyboard focus and scroll restoration. If a narrow state cannot support a secondary comparison, disclose the constraint and offer a usable alternative. Hiding half the data without explanation is not responsiveness.
